Master’s Thesis · Ongoing

Comparison of Vehicle Motion Simulation in Virtual Reality Across the Motion-Fidelity Continuum

This master’s thesis investigates how different levels of physical motion fidelity influence user experience and interaction in immersive vehicle simulation. The study compares four conditions spanning stationary virtual reality, compact motion simulation, room-scale motion simulation, and a real-vehicle reference.

Domain
Immersive HCI, VR/XR, human-vehicle interaction, automotive HCI, human factors
Status
Experimental design, system integration, and study preparation are ongoing
Institution
Ulm University

Research Motivation

Vehicle-motion simulators differ substantially in cost, physical footprint, movement capability, and ecological validity. This thesis examines whether lower-cost motion approaches can provide meaningful experiential and interaction benefits compared with stationary VR and a real-vehicle reference.

Research Focus

The project examines how motion-simulation approaches differ in perceived motion fidelity, presence, comfort, workload, simulator experience, and interaction performance.

Experimental Systems

  • Stationary VR
  • YawVR motion platform
  • VAMPIRE Chair room-scale condition
  • Real-car reference

Current Status

Experimental design, system integration, and study preparation are ongoing. Participant results and final analyses are not yet available.
