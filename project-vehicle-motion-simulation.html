Master’s thesis · Ongoing

Comparison of Vehicle Motion Simulation in Virtual Reality Across the Motion-Fidelity Continuum

My master’s thesis at Ulm University investigates vehicle-motion simulation in virtual reality across a planned continuum: stationary VR, two YawVR movement configurations, the VAMPIRE Chair, and an in-car reference. The research examines realism, presence, comfort, workload, and interaction during simulated driving. Participant data were collected for the VAMPIRE Chair (C3) condition; the thesis reports that condition’s results and discusses the broader planned comparisons.

Area

About the project

Vehicle-motion simulators differ greatly in cost, size, and the type of movement they can reproduce. This thesis looks at what changes when people experience the same virtual journey with no physical motion, with two different motion systems, and in a real moving vehicle.

The study is intended to show where lower-cost simulators are useful, where their limits become noticeable, and how physical motion affects both the overall experience and interaction during the ride.

Conditions being compared

Stationary VR

Participants see the virtual journey through a VR headset, but no physical vehicle motion is added.

YawVR motion platform

A compact motion platform adds rotational and tilt-based motion cues while the participant remains in VR.

VAMPIRE Chair

A powered room-scale chair moves through the available space to create physical motion cues during the virtual journey.

Real-car reference

A real moving car provides the reference condition for comparing the three simulator setups.

What the study examines

The comparison focuses on:

  • perceived realism and motion fidelity
  • presence
  • comfort and simulator experience
  • workload
  • interaction performance during the ride

Current status

Participant sessions were conducted in the VAMPIRE Chair (C3) condition. The thesis focuses its reported empirical results on this collected condition and explains the remaining planned conditions as part of the study design. It does not present uncollected cross-condition data as a completed comparison.

Technologies

Unity C# VR/XR Motion-simulation hardware Python Structured experimental logging Questionnaire systems Git

Study scope and next steps

The broader study design covers stationary VR, yaw-only and yaw-plus-roll YawVR setups, the VAMPIRE Chair, and an in-car reference. A full empirical comparison across those conditions would require additional participant data. This page will be updated with the thesis findings after submission.
